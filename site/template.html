Method

How the expected vote is computed

  • Expected vote. For each office and party, the ratio of the party's midterm vote to its presidential vote in the state two years earlier = an office-and-party baseline + an effect for holding the White House. The baseline is the state's own 1994–2022 history for that office. The effect is one number estimated from the 1994–2022 midterms of {{pool_names}}: {{b26}} points of the presidential vote for 2026. The 2026 expectation is the 2024 presidential vote times (baseline + effect for the party holding the White House). For 2018 and 2022 the year itself is left out.
  • Votes are the Republican and Democratic nominees' own party-line votes. Alaska since 2022 has no party nominees; there each party's vote is the combined first-choice vote of its candidates.
  • 2026 counts are unofficial. {{asof_states}}

How much the pool matters

The president's-party effect can be estimated from one state's history or from many. One state alone gives unstable numbers (Alaska's own history even gives the wrong sign), so the figures use every state with data. The table shows each state's main race under five choices: the gap between the actual vote and the adjusted expected vote, the effect b used, and how many races land on each side. {{pool_party_note}}
